PURPOSE

CRI governs AI as an organizational system rather than as a collection of
chatbots.

A control is considered implemented only when it has:

- a named human owner;
- a durable artifact;
- a gate or review cadence.

JUDGMENT AND ACCOUNTABILITY

No model or agent can:

- approve its own production promotion;
- expand its own permissions;
- accept its own risk;
- authorize a financial transfer;
- waive a failed security gate;
- waive a failed privacy gate.


Named humans remain accountable for those decisions.


CRI's highest unattended autonomy is limited to narrow, reversible,
low-blast-radius actions whose outcomes can be independently verified.


The decision to protect beneficiary data takes priority over convenience or
maximum automation.


REVIEW

The Director of IT chairs a quarterly AI governance review.

The review examines:

- new AI systems;
- incidents;
- model changes;
- tool changes;
- security findings;
- evaluation drift;
- cost;
- regulatory changes;
- policy exceptions.

A severe AI incident triggers an out-of-cycle review.
